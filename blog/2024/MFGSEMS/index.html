Organizing our Local Field Trip (MFGSEMS)

Created on April 22, 2024

2024   ·   planning,   organizing,   event   ·   volunteering

TARGET AUDIENCE: MEM_students, academe students planning local trips;

EXPERIENCE:

SITUATION: Here’s what happened: most of the batch decided to take their field trip overseas, with the department’s full approval and support. They had everything planned out since the previous term. For those of us who preferred to stay local, we were left with no choice but to organize our own field trip.

There were 13 of us, and we needed to find 7 local companies that could accommodate us. With the help of the department (specifically Doc Ira, who really took responsibility for us), we managed to bring that number down to 5 by counting local company visits on campus and online sessions as valid field trips. Two more companies were added to the list thanks to the original trip committee, with suggestions from batchmates Teo and Raph.

(I never got the chance to thank them back then, but THANK YOU TALAGA TT ❤️)

FINDING COMPANIES AND ORGANIZATIONS:

I have cold-called and emailed 20 manufacturing companies, organization and agencies on the south of Luzon with close proximity within Manila. I was able to narrow that down to 5 potential candidates for the field trip. Again, these were coming from favors on my end where I reached out to contacts within the industry. A very important thing to cross immediately is that if you are looking for technical field trips, government agencies should be the first thing you should consider! The NAST heritage center and DOST-based agencies, mainly in Bicutan, will happily offer field trips packages to the public and for free. Other companies are open to field trips but it will cost you.

Despite the hectic schedule of the term, I was able to secure the remaining 3 companies/institutions (with a +1 backup since we are already there) which were the 2 laboratory facilities of the DOST-MIRDC (AMCEN and AMERIAL), DOST-NAST and supposedly Schneider ELectric with their training facility in one of their plants upon back-and-forth discussions.

In the last minute, nagback-out both DOST-NAST and Schneider Electric due to conflicting schedules on their maintenance and plant schedule respectively. I was lowkey giving up on how we can get another company since we need one more. Doc Ira came to the rescue saying that she knows the someone in DOST-MIRDC (I believe it was a Section Director!!) and let us tour in the instrumentation and metrology lab to secure us that last company TT.

CLEARANCE AND TRANSPORTATION: Clearance for both medical and off-campus visit forms were a nightmare. There should be atmost one week grace period to submit all the necessary files. Along with it being notarized and my unfamiliarity with the forms doing this alone, it made us rush the medical to get it within the same day for all 13 students. The clinic expedited our request for a clearance with Doc Ira as the requesting personnel. Nainis nga samin dahil we had to rush it but how hard it is to check their database for this huhu

Another concern was the transportation. We were able to secure a UV van from the university through the proper channels but that was only for a one-day event and we were not able to secure another UV van for another trip scheduled on a different day. Thankfully, I know my way around Taft particularly on the UV express going to Bicutan. Tinanong ko ung kuya na nangongolekta ng bayad kung may available na UV van to accomodate us for a private trip that was scheduled tomorrow haha Thankfully meron naman and we agreed to a reasonable price going to Laguna and Manila back-forth whole day (5k). The rest of the events went smoothly with me giving the directions for field trip visit we were going in the south.

With these hail marys, I was able to organize and accomplish the 7 companies to visit for the requirements in MFGSEMS.

WHAT WE COULD IMPROVE ON: I think the issue was from the beginning with wrong expectations. I, atleast, thought that the department will be providing suggestions and organize the field trip but I was clueless to the process since I did not attend the orientation admittedly and also no one informed me of any actions needed right before the 3rd week of the term.

It was a roller-coaster knowing that I have to find companies but I also selfishly took the responsibility for myself. I know for a fact that the load may have been lighter if I have been more transparent to the local students taking this course too and let them help in the process which I only did later in the planning stage. I think that was also the reoccuring theme in my college journey where there is less communication within peers.

Overall, if there is a moral lesson out of this story, it would be the following:

  • Plan ahead and dont be fooled to wrong expectations to the college department. Think that you have to do the work still on securing companies. This is more important if you branch out from the majority taking the class.
  • Target immediately government agencies and secure a date for the field trip. Dont hesitate and call them directly if there is no followup after one week. Secure it ASAP since you need to fill out forms afterwards.
  • COmmunicate to fellow students taking the class and how you can tackle the field trip requirements. Thankfully we had Doc Ira who was super supportive and resourceful to get us the 7 companies to visit. You should also be the same to take an appropriate coordinator to help you (the students).

ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS: Doc Ira for taking care of us students na tuwaliwas sa batch-decision on the field trip (haha); Teo, Aids for helping us in the forms and securing local companies kahit alam ko hassle :”); Lastly, Raph for providing us a company to visit, we wouldnt be able to secure all 7 companies without them!!

Shoutout also to my fellow local peeps, ykwya! hope its cool i share this here!
